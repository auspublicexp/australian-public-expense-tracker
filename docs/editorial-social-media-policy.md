# Editorial and social-media policy

## Purpose

APET uses social media to highlight selected findings from already published official-data analysis. Social media is not the complete publication record and is not intended to turn every generated chart into a post.

The public website is the primary archive for APET charts, tables, methodology and supporting data. Social posts are a separate editorial layer used only when a chart or result is worth explaining with additional context.

## Selection principles

Before preparing a post or reply:

1. Confirm that the underlying APET chart or table has been generated from the intended official source and reporting period.
2. Check the relevant official source or record when a claim is significant, unusual or likely to be misunderstood.
3. Use terminology that matches what the source actually reports. For example, reported contract value and reported grant-award value should not be described as cash paid unless the source supports that interpretation.
4. Include material limitations or context when omitting them could change the meaning of the result.
5. Prefer findings that add useful factual context rather than posts created only to fill a schedule.

APET does not require every chart in a published dataset update to become a social post.

## Tone and neutrality

Posts and replies should remain calm, factual and politically neutral. APET should not use outrage-style framing, partisan advocacy, personal attacks, or language that implies expenditure is inherently good, bad, wasteful or justified.

Where the data cannot support a conclusion, the post should say less rather than speculate.

## Replies and engagement

Replies to public posts should be selective. A reply is appropriate when APET data can materially clarify, verify or add context to a claim about Australian public expenditure.

Do not reply merely to promote APET. Skip weak, abusive, highly partisan or purely argumentative engagement where a neutral data contribution is unlikely to improve the discussion.

## Drafting and publication

Chart-generation scripts should not automatically publish social posts. Drafting, context checking and final publication remain separate steps.

Automated tools may help identify candidate stories, prepare factual summaries or flag relevant public claims, but a human review should confirm the source, wording, context and link before publication.

APET does not promise a fixed social-media publishing cadence. Quality, source verification and useful context take priority over posting frequency.
